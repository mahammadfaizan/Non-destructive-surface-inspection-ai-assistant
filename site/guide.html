BUILD YOUR OWN · DOCUMENT-GROUNDED (RAG) INSPECTION ASSISTANT

Make one for your standard in an afternoon

Your documents. Your acceptance criteria. Our example prompt. About 1–2 hours from sign-up to first tested answer.

← Back to the live A108 assistant
Before you start: use only AI tools your company has approved, and only standards you hold a license for. Never upload export-controlled, proprietary or customer data to a public AI tool. The assistant gives guidance; a qualified Level II / III makes the call.
1

Set up an AI (LLM) account

Pick a tool that can hold custom instructions plus your own documents in one reusable workspace. That pairing is what makes it a document-grounded (RAG) assistant: it retrieves answers from your files instead of from memory.

  • Claude (claude.ai): create a Project, then add instructions and files to it.
  • ChatGPT (chatgpt.com): create a custom GPT, or a Project, with instructions and files.
  • Microsoft Copilot or another enterprise tool: use whichever "agent" or "workspace" feature your IT team has enabled.

Sign up with your work email if your company has an enterprise plan. Feature names and plan limits change, so check the tool's help pages if a menu looks different.

2

Gather your documents

  • The licensed standard (PDF) that holds your acceptance table, for example ASTM A108, ASME Section V, or AWS D1.1.
  • Any companion standard the table depends on. For A108 this is A29, for grade sulfur ranges.
  • Your SOP, work instruction or cheat sheet, so answers match how your shop works.

Keep it small: only the documents the assistant needs. Fewer files means fewer wrong lookups.

3

Paste the example prompt

Copy this into the workspace's instructions field. Replace every [BRACKETED] item with your own details, starting with your assistant's name.

EXAMPLE PROMPT · BASED ON OUR A108 ASSISTANT 
<role>
You are [ASSISTANT NAME], a quality-engineering assistant for [PRODUCT, e.g. cold finished steel bar].
You interpret [STANDARD, e.g. ASTM A108-18] [TOPIC, e.g. surface inspection] requirements.
</role>

<source_material>
[LIST THE UPLOADED FILES, e.g. ASTM A108-18 (Table A1.8), ASTM A29-20 (Tables 1–2), Shop SOP-123]
</source_material>

<rules>
1. Answer ONLY from <source_material>. Never use memory for limits.
2. Quote the table row/column and footnote you used, by label.
3. If the needed [grade, size, condition] isn't in the source, say
   "Not specified in the provided text", list what's missing, and ask
   the user for the specific details needed.
4. Never interpolate between [size ranges] unless the source says to.
5. Keep units exactly as written; state any conversion separately.
6. Don't give accept/reject decisions as final. Flag them as
   "per the cited table; confirm against your engineering criteria."
</rules>

<input>
Product: [e.g. grade 1045]
Size/shape: [e.g. 1 in. round]
Finish/condition: [e.g. cold drawn]
Discontinuity: [type, measured depth, method e.g. eddy current]
</input>

<output_format>
- Applicable table row/column:
- Tolerance value (with units):
- Footnotes/conditions that apply:
- Result: acceptable / rejectable / cannot determine
- Assumptions or missing info:
</output_format>
4

Upload your documents and add examples

  1. Upload the files from step 2 to the workspace's knowledge or files area.
  2. Add 2–3 worked input → output examples in the same format as the prompt. Examples teach the format faster than rules do.
  3. Save the workspace and give it the name you chose.
5

Test it before anyone relies on it

Write 10 questions whose answers you already know, checked by hand against the standard. Include the hard ones:

Test typeWhat a good answer does
Straight lookupGives the right limit and cites the row, column and footnote
Clearly over / underSays rejectable / acceptable, with the math
Missing detailsSays "Not specified" and asks for exactly what's missing
Impossible inputFlags it (e.g. "10 % sulfur" for a table that stops at 0.35 %)
Out of scopeRefuses to guess outside the table's range

Any wrong answer: tighten the rule it broke, add an example, and re-run all 10.

6

Optional: put a calculator on the web with GitHub

Once your rules are fixed, you can turn them into a small web page like ours that works on any phone.

  1. Create a free account at github.com.
  2. Click New repository, give it a name, and make it Public.
  3. Click Add file → Upload files and upload your page files (an index.html at minimum). Commit.
  4. Open Settings → Pages, choose to deploy from your main branch, and save.
  5. After a minute or two, your link appears at the top of that page. Turn it into a QR code for your team.

Do not put licensed standard text or tables in a public repository. Keep the standard inside your company's AI workspace.

7

Keep a human in the loop

  • Traceable: every answer cites its reference criteria.
  • Bounded: it answers only from your documents, and asks when unsure.
  • Accountable: the inspector cross-checks every result before it reaches the record or an NCR.
